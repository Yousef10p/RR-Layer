Batch Normalization using an SVD Layer

Abdulmohsen Mohammedsaleh  •  Majed Alsulami  •  Yousef Alogiley
Mentor: Jad Mounayer  •  KAUST Academy Summer Program 2026

01. Introduction

Batch Normalization (BN) rescales every feature of a mini-batch to mean 0 and variance 1:

y = γ · (x − μB) / σB + β

This simple step lets networks train faster and reach higher accuracy, making BN a standard layer in deep learning.

02. Objective

Rank Reduction Autoencoders (Mounayer et al., 2024) regularize networks with the truncated SVD, no extra loss needed. Like BN, it changes the statistics of the batch.

Can the SVD alone normalize a batch as well as BN? We build it into a standalone SVD layer to find out.

03. Methodology

The SVD layer keeps the batch’s top-k singular directions and normalizes them by their singular values:

X = U Σ VT  →  X̂ = √N · Uk VkT   (σi → 1)
  • Task: regress a Gaussian source’s centre (x, y) from a 64×64 image — point-source localization, as in astrometry and microscopy.
  • Compare Plain vs BN vs SVD from identical initial weights, over up to 32 seeds — sweeping rank k, noise level and type, and dataset size N.
  • External validity via source injection: a known source planted on real image backgrounds (astronomy’s own validation method).

04. Results

  • SVD beats BN in every like-for-like bottleneck comparison — often by orders of magnitude — though per-layer BN on clean, plentiful data remains stronger.
  • Beats the plain net 2–3× when data is scarce and noisy (N ≤ 100); ties at N ≈ 200.
  • Frozen inference basis is free (0.0028 vs 0.0034 MSE); per-layer insertion diverges — use one bottleneck.

05. Conclusion

  • The SVD layer is a more robust drop-in alternative to BN, and outperforms even the unregularized network in the small-data, high-noise regime of source localization.
  • Its one weakness: keeping top-k passes structured low-rank nuisance through — the inverse of astronomy’s KLIP. A reject-top-k variant is the natural next step.
